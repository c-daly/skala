## Fit details that affect substitutions

- **Bearing cap screws:** H8 is not H2. Use a flat DIN 965 Phillips head no larger than Ø3.8 mm; ISO 10642 socket countersunk M2 heads can be larger and are not cleared by this cap. The 6 mm overall screw gives about 4 mm engagement in the printed 5 mm-deep pilot. Heads must seat flush; a proud head can consume the 1 mm carrier gap. Use a short PH0 driver, no powered driver. Printed threads and cap strength require physical qualification.
- **Bearing and hub version:** B1 is a 626 bearing. The original hub used 608 bearings (8 × 22 × 7 mm); those bearings and that hub are not substitutes for the current 626/M6 assembly. Identify older prints and stock using [assembly step 3](p0-assembly.md#3-assemble-the-idler-hub-and-axle) before buying or reprinting. A correctly identified 626 that does not seat still requires a printed-fit correction; bearing size alone does not qualify the fit.
- **Axle:** the current BSH6X50 supplier data specifies a 26 ± 5 mm plain shank. Both bearings occupy the first 15 mm beneath the head; the nut begins about 40.5 mm beneath it. The listed 21–31 mm plain range therefore covers both bearing bores while allowing the nut to tighten. Do not use a fully threaded M6×50 or a low/button-head variant. Check the delivered shank, bearing slip fit and free rotation when clamped.
- **Washers:** the M4 washers must be Ø9 outside. A Ø12 M4 washer collides with the bracket foot. The M6 washer must be Ø12 to suit its shallow seat; the thread size alone does not specify its outside diameter.
- **M4 bolt threading:** full or partial threading is acceptable for H3. With a partially threaded bolt, the plain section and thread runout must end before the nut, whose bearing face is nominally 17.6 mm beneath the bolt head. The nut must clamp the joint and engage its nylon insert. Only A1 specifically requires a plain shank for the bearings; the linked M4 listing does not specify its threaded length.
- **Heads and stacks:** the selected M3 heads can be Ø5.68 and M4 heads Ø7.22, slightly larger than the CAD's nominal Ø5.5/Ø7. They were checked against the installed lower geometry without collisions. A Ø10.22 × 6 M6 head also clears the retained hub. The M2 Ø4 × 1.6 pan-head envelope clears the vendor wheel model. Supplier washer thickness ranges are 0.45–0.55 mm (M3), 0.7–0.9 mm (M4), and 1.4–1.8 mm (M6); with nominal printed dimensions, even the maximum thicknesses leave the listed bolts projecting beyond their locknuts. Physical checks still need to confirm complete nylon-ring engagement and access.
- **Wheel screws:** keep the specified 4 mm length and pan head. The wheel's nominal 2 mm mounting plate leaves about 2 mm engagement. Do not substitute countersunk screws, tapping screws or longer screws without checking the actual joint.

Use the [assembly guide](p0-assembly.md) for hardware placement and acceptance checks. Its tools list covers 5, 3 and 2.5 mm hex keys, matching nut tools, crosspoint drivers, calipers and an outer-race bearing seating tool.

## Later flipper materials

These are **excluded from current S1 order quantities**. The [joint-option register](../../cad/actuator_options.json) owns three main cost tiers: budget DIY BLDC/cycloidal, integrated brushless joint, and aspirational high-end Dynamixel. Reduced XL430 and brushed encoder gearmotor remain additional budget comparators. It includes associated parts, allowances and sourced prices with their dates; no option is approved for purchase.

Bare-BLDC joints add one independent three-phase driver channel per positioning axis. Two pitch joints need two channels; four pitch joints need four; an eventual four-pitch/four-lateral robot needs eight if all use bare BLDCs. A combined MCU/driver includes local processing; a SimpleFOC Shield requires a compatible MCU as well. Include motor/output feedback, gearbox bearings/metal pins/fasteners, supported hubs, board mounts/cooling, wiring and power/stop changes. Separate encoder breakouts are boards too. Integrated brushless joints and Dynamixels contain their motor drivers; command-interface hardware, mounts and power distribution remain additional items.

The proposed cheap motor/driver/encoder subtotal is not a complete joint price. Use the [whole-robot fit and power screen](../design/physical-design-spec.md#bldc-fit-and-power-screen) before comparing costs, and include all [affected structure, electronics and software](../design/physical-design-spec.md#if-bldc-cycloidal-pitch-is-selected). S1's battery, proposed fuse/relay and deck layout are not already sized or qualified for four pitch controllers. Selection should preserve current parts where practical, but the cost of any displaced parts belongs to the alternative.

The [form-factor table](../design/physical-design-spec.md#form-factor-choices-and-joint-counts) gives the counts for each development stage. A two-track/two-pitch vehicle retains two propulsion motors, while a fixed main pair plus a flipper pair uses four propulsion motors despite having only two pitch joints. The ultimate robot needs four pitch and four separately sized lateral assemblies under the one-lateral-axis assumption; do not simply double the pitch BOM. Count each shared interface/power assembly once, then resize it for the selected stage.
